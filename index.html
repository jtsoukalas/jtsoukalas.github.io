§ 1

Research

Doctoral work since 2026

More of the software we build shapes daily life, often with AI inside. One question keeps coming up: will these systems act the way we agree they should, following a shared moral compass? The question is vague, and so are most answers. That's why we aim to treat responsibility as something we can measure.

In practice it gets harder. Projects keep growing while deadlines keep shrinking, and legislation like the “EU AI Act” now asks teams to show responsibility, not just claim it.

So how do you tell how responsible a system is? My research starts at the beginning: design. In agile teams, where things change all the time, the aim is to check each sprint for things like fairness, safety and transparency before it ships, so speed doesn't cost responsibility.

It is a practical question for me too. My BSc thesis became the HR management system (HRMS) Harokopio University of Athens runs on, and my MSc thesis extended it with AI, so I know the pull between shipping fast and shipping responsibly first-hand.

Research project

    Certification for Ethical and Regulatory Transparency in Artificial Intelligence · Horizon Europe

    An EU project building tools and guidelines that help AI systems meet European rules: checking data quality, measuring bias and protecting personal data, on the way to certifying AI. I work on it with Prof. Mara Nikolaidou and her team at the Department of Informatics and Telematics.

Interests

  • Responsible systems by design

    Fairness, safety and transparency treated as design requirements rather than after-the-fact checks.

  • Agile development lifecycles

    Fitting responsibility checks into iterative, fast-moving development without stalling it.

  • Metrics for responsibility

    Ways to measure responsibility, compare it, and check it over a system's life.

  • Cloud computing and AI systems

    The two settings in focus, where shared infrastructure and automated decisions raise the stakes.

    Jason Tsoukalas · Supervisor: Prof. Thomas Kamalakis

More

  1. 2025

    Mass email campaign platform

    A personal summer project for the University's PR office and secretaries. You connect a Gmail account over OAuth 2.0 and upload a CSV or XLSX spreadsheet with one row per recipient, which you can edit in the browser. Each column becomes a variable you can drop into the message body, and each recipient can get their own attachments. Messages go out through the Gmail API as background jobs, with retries, throttling and a delivery log for every message.
